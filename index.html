Rentletter

AI application sorter and lead generator for realtors

Active

Software for rental realtors in Ontario and BC. It sorts applications with AI, and the strong applicants who lost the unit become leads.

Who it’s for
Rental realtors
Stage
Free and pre-revenue
Outcome
Realtors join weekly
Live at
rentletter.ca ↗
Why I built it
I wanted to build in real estate before I knew what. This is the fourth version, and every change came from a realtor conversation, not a hunch. The product I planned and the product they use share almost nothing, which is the point.
Who it’s for
Rental realtors, plus the landlords and tenants on the other side of every application. A realtor will tolerate a tool, but a tenant abandons anything that asks them to learn. So it has to be obvious on first contact, on a phone, to a stranger.
How it went
Free and pre-revenue, deliberately. The goal is onboarding, not billing. New realtors join weekly, early users stay, and that is the only metric I trust.
What blocked it
Nothing technical. The open question is market entry: which slice to go at first, and how. I would rather solve that with conversations than a guess.
Where it stands
Active, and where most of my time goes. So far, so good.

Why I built it
Asymmetric bets: risk a couple hundred, make a couple thousand, one hit pays for nine misses. Nothing I could buy found those candidates ahead of the market, so I built it. The name is ridiculous on purpose, it was never for sale.
Who it’s for
Me. That’s the whole user research. Building for one user means no onboarding, no persuasion, and features that earn their place on a Tuesday.

05 / Feeling

Echoes

Save a song to a place

Live

Save a song to the place you heard it. A timeline and a map of where music happened to you.

by Melomaniac Studios

Who it’s for
People who relate to music this way
Stage
Active development
Outcome
Friends kept using it
Live at
echoesbymm.com ↗
Why I built it
I left home seven years ago and have moved a lot since. A song puts me back in a place, closer than any photo. You keep every photo and none of the songs, so I built the thing that keeps them.
Who it’s for
It started as mine, then friends kept using it. Now it’s a small, quiet social space for people who relate to music this way. I assumed that was a niche of one, and it isn’t.
How it went
It’s live, and what people save is more personal than I expected to be trusted with. That shaped the product: no likes, no counts, nothing that turns a memory into a performance.
What blocked it
Spotify closed off API access, the kind of failure that can end a project. I rebuilt around audio recognition, so the app now identifies songs from the room itself. That made it better and open to everyone, but I never want that exposure again.
Where it stands
Live at echoesbymm.com, in active development, and the project I care about most.

I don’t like DJing. Offered a Toronto slot, I wrote my own music and played only that. Most DJs don’t make what they play, and most musicians can’t play what they make. So I doubled down: every set written from scratch for its night.

A Toronto audience that mostly knows DJ sets. From across a room the two look identical, so I taught the audience the difference beforehand. Teaching people to receive the thing is part of making the thing.
